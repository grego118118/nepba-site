Meet your teacher

Maggie d'Amour

"I learned to listen long before I learned to speak — I listened to birds. The hermit thrush at dawn. The chickadees in winter. I built this studio so that you, too, could come home to that quieter place."

Maggie is a 500-hour Yoga Alliance certified teacher, lifelong birder, and founder of Birdsong Yoga. After fifteen years of practice across India, the Berkshires, and the wetlands of western Mass, she opened the studio in 2026 with a simple intention: a slower kind of strength.

Every class is taught with patience and precision. Every retreat is shaped by the seasons. And every Friday morning, weather permitting, she leads a free walk-and-watch from the studio steps to the meadow.

What we offer

Classes & Practices

Six signature offerings — each one shaped by the rhythm of the Pioneer Valley. Browse the full schedule below or book a single class to begin.

Sunrise Birdsong Flow

60 min · All levels

The signature class. A gently warming vinyasa that begins as the dawn chorus does — with one bright note. Tuesdays and Saturdays at 6:30am.

Slow & Steady Vinyasa

75 min · All levels

A grounded, breath-led flow with longer holds and intelligent alignment. Good for new and returning students.

Restorative & Yin

90 min · All levels

Deep, supported postures with bolsters and blankets. A long, quiet practice for the nervous system. Sunday evenings.

Strong & Steady

60 min · Level 2

A heated, more athletic flow for students with an established practice. Inversions and arm balances by request.

Outdoor Flow at Arcadia

75 min · April–October

Once a month we practice on the meadow at Arcadia Wildlife Sanctuary. Bring a mat. The herons, warblers and bobolinks bring the rest. $25 · includes sanctuary admission.

Workshops & Retreats

A slower season,
in good company.

Twice a year we close the studio doors and travel — once in spring for the warbler migration, once in autumn for the foliage and the geese. All retreats include lodging, meals, two daily practices and guided walks led by Maggie and a local naturalist.
